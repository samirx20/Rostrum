# Listening without ears: the look tools

You cannot hear the render, so each look tool turns one aspect of it into numbers or a picture. Render first
(`render_mix`, then `inspect_job` until it is done; or `run_script` with `look`, which writes, renders and checks in
one call); every look tool reads the last render unless you pass `wav`.
Two read the document instead and need no render: `look_arrangement`, and `look_music` (`what: "harmony"`) on a song
with notes written.

| Question | Tool | What to read |
|---|---|---|
| Is every part where I meant it, before rendering? Which tracks start on each marker? | `look_arrangement` | a picture of the written song (a row per track: notes by pitch, drum hits, clips; bars, sections, markers) and, per marker, the tracks that start on it and each track's nearest start in ms and beats. Reads the document, so no render is needed |
| How loud is it, and does it build where it should? Where is the loudest moment? | `look_sound` (`what: "loudness"`) | integrated LUFS, true peak, the loudest moment and its time (`loudest`: the loudest 3 s and 0.4 s windows with where each starts and ends, and the highest sample with its time; the summary says them), the short-term curve (a sag or dip shows as a valley in the picture; each point is the 3 s ending there, so the curve peaks up to 3 s after the loud moment: read the time from `loudest`), clipped moments, the `ending` line (last 0.5 s level, still above -50 dBFS?, last 50 ms dropping faster than the fade = a cut, and the audible end: where the sound falls under -60 dBFS for good), the loudness of each section (`sections`, whole song only; `start` / `end` measure one stretch), where the limiter worked and which tracks drove it, and the room for speech (`speech_room`: how many dB a voice at the mix's loudness stands over the music in 1–4 kHz; aim for +6 dB or more under a voice-over) |
| The render landed under the loudness target: what exactly do I change? | `edit_loudness` (a job; it re-renders offline to search) | the exact edits, ready for `edit_document`: `keep_balance` (the `maxLimit` that reaches the target, no track moves), `tame_peaks` (a gentle compressor on the most peaked track, threshold and makeup measured: the target inside the master's `maxLimit`, balance kept) and `trim_track` (the smallest cut of the most peaked track that reaches it inside the master's `maxLimit`), and when it needs over ~3 dB of limiting `lift_quiet` (the quiet passages ridden up on the master's volume lane, measured); `{"apply": "best"}` makes the one it names best |
| Is one track buried or too loud? | `look_sound` (`what: "levels"`) | each stem's LUFS, dB under the loudest / the mix, and the track's volume in dB (move a track N dB: volume + N); flags (a float stem over 0 dBFS is held, not clipped) |
| Do sounds land on time (a hit on a cut, a footstep on a frame)? | `look_music` (`what: "events"`) with `expect` | each expected time's nearest onset and offset in ms; pass within `tolerance`; with `by_stem`, which stem hits each time |
| Does each moment come across, not just land? What would a listener complain about? | `look_music` (`what: "events"`) or `look_sound` (`what: "loudness"`) with `expect` moments: `{"at": 31.5, "kind": "hit"}` (kinds `hit`, `stop`, `drop`, `climax`, `lift`, `entry`, `voice` with `end`), and the brief's tempo as `{"kind": "tempo", "bpm": 132}` (no `at`) | `faults`, each with its time, the measured number and what to change (see *Faults* below). `look_sound` loudness also names the whole-piece faults every time, with or without `expect` |
| Is there hum, a whistle, or masking? | `look_sound` (`what: "spectrum"`) (with `marks`) | `hum_lines` are the steady lines that are unwanted (flat level, on two or more multiples of 50 or 60 Hz together; a lone held note, even a very low one, is a tone, not hum); `tones` lists every steady line with its note name, so a held note shows as a note, not hum; marks show where events should be |
| Does it lean left or right, or fall apart in mono? | `look_sound` (`what: "stereo"`) | balance per section, correlation, mono warnings |
| Is there boom where the picture has none, or mud? | `look_sound` (`what: "low_end"`) | each track's and the mix's share under 150 Hz, under 60 Hz and in the low mids (150–500 Hz, where parts piling up sound muddy) |
| What is the tempo, is it steady, do sounds sit on the beat or the document's tempo map? | `look_music` (`what: "beats"`) (`stems: true` for every stem) | on a render, the document's tempo checked against the audio: "124 BPM, the document's; the audio's beats fit it" (or "do NOT fit it", with what they read). A groove also repeats at half, double, 2/3 and 3/2 of its tempo, so the reading from the sound alone (`heard_bpm`) can name a related tempo; the summary says so. With `wav` (no document), the audio's own BPM, and a close call names the other reading. Also `steadiness`, beat and downbeat guess, per onset the ms from the nearest 1/4 beat (+ = late) against the audio's own beats and the document's grid (swung where the document swings); `swing`: the swing set (each track's feel) next to the swing heard, on the same 0 … 1 scale ("swing set 0.55 on 8ths (drums), heard 0.56"); picture: waveform, beats, onsets coloured green / orange / red |
| Is a melody on the right notes, in time and in tune? Are a chord part's notes all there? | `look_music` (`what: "pitch"`) with `track` | every written note (each note of a chord too) judged on its attack, what is new in the sound just after it starts, so a ringing harp, a pedalled piano or a reverb tail never reads as extra, missing or late notes: ok / wrong_octave / missing, and on a one-voice part also late / early (from the sound's onsets), out_of_tune and wrong_note (from the pitch line). The first line says the register in plain words: "bass sounds where written (68 of 70 notes heard in their octave)" or "bass sounds one octave lower than written on 54 of 69 notes (written D3, heard D2): the instrument's samples, or your octave". Wrong octave is called only when the partials clearly say so (a low note's strong 2nd harmonic is not mistaken for it). Plus the pitch line's notes (name, cents off, vibrato, drift): on a ringing part it holds more notes than were written; those are tails and are not counted. What it can't tell: a written note that is an overtone of a note sounding with it (an octave or a twelfth above) reads as there. Picture: the pitch line over the written notes, a dot per written note |
| Do two tracks fight for the same frequencies? | `look_sound` (`what: "clash"`) (needs stems) | spans ranked by how far one track buries the other (`buried_track`, `buried_by_db`, at least `min_over_db` 3): when, which Hz band, which two tracks, dB each stands over the other; an empty list is "no real problem" (equal levels in a band are not a clash); picture: time × band map coloured by pair. The floor follows the mix (reply's `floor_db`); tighten with `within_db` 3 and a `floor_db` 10 dB higher if a dense mix lights up everywhere |
| What key and chords is it in, and are the written notes playable and clean? | `look_music` (`what: "harmony"`) (no render needed: it reads the written notes; only a song with no notes, `source: "audio"` or a `wav` reads audio) | key (the song's own key when it has one, and the summary says so: "Key D minor (the song's key)"; only without one, the notes' or audio's estimate), chord per bar or beat, and plain-fact checks on the written notes: out of the instrument's range, close intervals under the low limit, voices crossing, upper voices over an octave apart, notes out of key (the summary names them: track, note, how many, from when). Each bar is judged against the key in force there (the document's key list: a song in G, then A, then G is checked in each; the summary names them, each chord row has its `key`), and a note of the coming key in the bar just before a key change (its dominant, say) is listed as leading into it (`checks.leads_into_key`), not as out of key |

Read the numbers as measurements: the engine compensates recorded instruments' attacks so a note at a marker is heard at it, and `inspect_instrument` gives each one's `attackMs`; onset detection itself reads a soft sound some ms after it starts (measured 1–11 ms for brass and harp, more for a very soft start) (a sound already playing at 0 s counts as an onset at 0), the chord from audio is a best
match to common shapes (a mix with drums and a melody on top reads rougher than the notes written in the document), a
key estimate cannot always tell a major key from its relative minor (the tool says when both fit), and the chord check
reads a written note as there when it is also an overtone of a lower note sounding (an octave or a twelfth above it), so
a note played an octave low passes; a very low note (under about E2) can read missing when its sample has little
fundamental: listen there before changing it.

## Faults: what a listener would complain about

Landing within 40 ms is not the same as being heard. Give the moments the brief asks for with their kind and read
`faults` (the summary says them first). Each names the time, the number and the usual fixes; it is measured, so a
drone meant as a drone may stay, but say why in your report.

| Fault | Measured | Usual fixes |
|---|---|---|
| weak hit / stop / entry at 31.50 s | the hit's attack in the finished, limited file (the rise over ~21 ms, every frequency counted alike, as the ear hears a strike) against the piece's typical onset ("1.0x a normal note", rank among all onsets); under 2.5x or not in the top 3 (drums coming back: under 2x). Beside it: its **bright attack** (2 kHz up), its strength against the notes of the 2 s before, and its loudness jump over the 1.5 s before | the fix follows the short number: bright attack low → a short loud part with a fast bright attack on it (crash, snare, a high piano or brass stab; a low boom alone reads as a normal note); jump small → silence or duck the bed 0.3–0.5 s before; the notes before strike as hard → shorter bed notes into it, nothing on the beat before; more instruments across registers. When the build already peaks at the ceiling (its highest sample in the 3 s before within 1.5 dB of the file's, at -3 dBFS or more) the list starts with **headroom**: nothing else can make the hit louder |
| no hit at … | nothing starts within 80 ms | put the notes on the time; `by_stem` names the nearest part |
| stop lands soft | a `stop` whose attack is not clearly the band's strongest arrival (under 2.5x a normal note or not in the top 3; an onset in the second before at 70 % of its strength or more), or whose next second is not clearly quieter (the level falls under 8 dB); on a render, how far the master limiter pulls around it | rest the band on the beat before, put its strongest accent on the stop, end held notes, ride and pedal at once, short notes on the stop itself. When the limiter pulls 3 dB or more there, the fix **leads with taming the peaks**: a compressor on the track driving the limiter (named; `edit_loudness {"apply": "tame_peaks", "track": …}`) or lower velocity on ride and cymbals, and only then a higher `maxLimit` (a ride forcing 8.7 dB of limiting left a jazz stop at 1.2x) |
| silent start (whole piece) | the first 20 ms over -45 dBFS comes later than 0.25 s | start the music at once. To land a beat on a sync time, play a pickup before bar 1 with `downbeat(bar(1), at=<seconds>)` or place the sync moment in seconds; never leave silence to push the music later |
| tempo far from the brief's (`{"kind": "tempo", "bpm": …}`) | the song's start tempo and the tempo it holds for most of its length (a short `land` glide or a pickup does not count) against the asked tempo; off by more than 0.5 % | keep the asked tempo and land the sync moment another way: `downbeat(bar(n), at=<seconds>)` (a pickup before bar 1), `land(bar(n), at=<seconds>, ramp_from=bar(m))` (a short tempo glide that arrives on time), or the moment in seconds. A bent tempo is heard as rushed or dragging |
| drums drop early | the last beat before a `drop` more than 1.5 beat-gaps (and 0.35 s) before it | a last hit or fill ending on the time |
| weak climax | the climax's downbeat read as a hit (the same numbers and fixes) | as a weak hit |
| build at the ceiling (hit, climax) | the build's highest sample in the 3 s before within 1.5 dB of the file's loudest, which is at -3 dBFS or more: the limiter's ceiling is already used, so the moment cannot be louder | keep the build's peaks 4–6 dB under the ceiling (its velocities or volumes, not the moment's) and let the moment ring or hold 3–5 s, so it carries the loudness target |
| climax cut short | how many seconds after the downbeat each 1 s stays at least as loud as the 3 s before; under 3 s | let it hold 3–5 s (held chords, ringing low notes, a cymbal swell) and settle over several seconds; a held climax carries the loudness target, so the build can start low |
| loudest moment after / before the climax | where the loudest 0.4 s starts, against a `climax`: late over 3 s (an orchestra blooming 1–2 s after a tutti downbeat is normal; keep that bloom), early over 0.5 s | late: hold the last bars before it a little lower, the strongest attack on the downbeat; early: hold the build's last bars back |
| lift barely lifts | the 3 s after a `lift` against the 3 s before, under +2 LU | add parts, open a filter, thin the bars before |
| busy speech band | room for speech in a `voice` span under +6 dB | low-pass pads and keys lower, dip 2–4 kHz |
| one harmony held (whole piece) | the same pitch classes every second for 12 s or more with the level moving under 12 dB: heard as one flat tone | change the chord or bass note every few bars, a moving inner line, let the sound open (filter, swells, a second texture) |
| near-mono mix (whole piece) | left/right correlation over 0.9 (wide mixes 0.4–0.8) | give a mono source its own left and right: a `chorus` effect with `spread` 1, a `tremolo` with `stereo` above 0 (1 = auto-pan) or a stereo reverb; pan the mono parts apart (keys a little one way, a second part the other; the kits are already spread from the audience's view); stereo instruments. `width` cannot widen a mono recording |
| dull top end (whole piece) | 4–16 kHz against 250 Hz–1 kHz in the brightest tenth of the playing frames, under -20 dB (benchmark: bright pop and lo-fi -2 to -9, orchestral and dark cues -13 to -19) | a bright part up an octave, higher velocity on recorded instruments (their loud layers are brighter), a cymbal, glockenspiel or bell at an audible level, fewer parts piled up in 250 Hz–1 kHz |
| top end did not grow (`climax`, `lift`) | the same ratio over the first 5 s that play against the 5 s from the moment; grew under +6 dB (builds that open up: +11 to +15) | add parts in register order (high ones last), raise recorded instruments' velocity through the build, a cymbal swell or a high octave into the moment |
| hit squashed by the limiter (render only) | the master's limiter held a moment down 3 dB or more; how far the 10 s before sat under it | a short loud moment cannot carry the loudness target, so the build does: let the hit ring or hold 3–6 s (a held chord, ringing low notes, a cymbal) and keep the build's peaks 4–6 dB under the ceiling. `edit_loudness` `lift_quiet` narrows the range: not the fix here |

"No faults found" means none of these, not that the music is good.

`render_mix` runs the same check on every finished mix: its reply lists the three weakest moments against the asked
ones (`expect`, or the song's markers named hit, stop, drums out, drums back, climax or lift), one line each with the
time, the measured numbers and the first fix (`data.weakest`). Name the moments as markers once and every render is
checked. A line that says "comes across" is a margin, not a fault. A missing moment comes first, then a silent start or
a bent tempo (heard from the first second), then the weakest moments, then the other whole-piece faults.

The quickest loop is one call: write the whole piece as one script and run it with `look` (`true`, or render_mix's
arguments: `{"look": {"name": "cue", "expect": [{"at": 31.5, "kind": "stop"}, {"kind": "tempo", "bpm": 132}]}}`).
The script merges, the mix renders and the reply is the script's line followed by render_mix's whole listening summary
(loudness, the weakest moments with their fixes, the files; `data.render`). Edit the script and run it again under the
same `name` with `look` until the list is clean. A failing script renders nothing; a render over 150 s answers its
job id for `inspect_job`.

What these cannot tell you: whether an instrument sounds real or sampled, whether phrasing is musical, whether a
recording sounds like the object on screen. Say so in your report; a person decides those by listening.

## Effects against a picture

- `look_video` `what: "sheet"` to see the whole clip, `"motion"` for sudden-stop candidates (impacts), `"frames"`
  around a time to check one event frame by frame.
- `inspect_library` (`what: "cuts"`) cuts recordings into single sounds with facts (peak, attack, brightness, low-end share), cached,
  so you can pick a cut by its measurements and place it with a clip (`in` = the cut's start).
- After placing sounds, `look_music` (`what: "events"`) with the event times as `expect` checks sync within a frame; give
  the big ones a kind (`{"at": 12.5, "kind": "hit"}`) and the faults say whether each stands out.
