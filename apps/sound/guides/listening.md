# Listening without ears: the look tools

You cannot hear the render, so each look tool turns one aspect of it into numbers or a picture. Render first
(`render_mix`, then `inspect_job` until it is done); every look tool reads the last render unless you pass `wav`.
Two read the document instead and need no render: `look_arrangement`, and `look_music` (`what: "harmony"`) on a song
with notes written.

| Question | Tool | What to read |
|---|---|---|
| Is every part where I meant it, before rendering? Which tracks start on each marker? | `look_arrangement` | a picture of the written song (a row per track: notes by pitch, drum hits, clips; bars, sections, markers) and, per marker, the tracks that start on it and each track's nearest start in ms and beats. Reads the document, so no render is needed |
| How loud is it, and does it build where it should? Where is the loudest moment? | `look_sound` (`what: "loudness"`) | integrated LUFS, true peak, the loudest moment and its time (`loudest`: the loudest 3 s and 0.4 s windows with where each starts and ends, and the highest sample with its time; the summary says them), the short-term curve (a sag or dip shows as a valley in the picture; each point is the 3 s ending there, so the curve peaks up to 3 s after the loud moment: read the time from `loudest`), clipped moments, the `ending` line (last 0.5 s level, still above -50 dBFS?, last 50 ms dropping faster than the fade = a cut), where the limiter worked and which tracks drove it, and the room for speech (`speech_room`: how many dB a voice at the mix's loudness stands over the music in 1–4 kHz; aim for +6 dB or more under a voice-over) |
| The render landed under the loudness target: what exactly do I change? | `edit_loudness` (a job; it re-renders offline to search) | the exact edits, ready for `edit_document`: `keep_balance` (the `maxLimit` that reaches the target, no track moves), `tame_peaks` (a gentle compressor on the most peaked track, threshold and makeup measured: the target inside the master's `maxLimit`, balance kept) and `trim_track` (the smallest cut of the most peaked track that reaches it inside the master's `maxLimit`); `{"apply": "best"}` makes the one it names best |
| Is one track buried or too loud? | `look_sound` (`what: "levels"`) | each stem's LUFS, dB under the loudest / the mix, and the track's volume in dB (move a track N dB: volume + N); flags (a float stem over 0 dBFS is held, not clipped) |
| Do sounds land on time (a hit on a cut, a footstep on a frame)? | `look_music` (`what: "events"`) with `expect` | each expected time's nearest onset and offset in ms; pass within `tolerance`; with `by_stem`, which stem hits each time |
| Is there hum, a whistle, or masking? | `look_sound` (`what: "spectrum"`) (with `marks`) | `hum_lines` are the steady lines that are unwanted (flat level, on two or more multiples of 50 or 60 Hz together; a lone held note, even a very low one, is a tone, not hum); `tones` lists every steady line with its note name, so a held note shows as a note, not hum; marks show where events should be |
| Does it lean left or right, or fall apart in mono? | `look_sound` (`what: "stereo"`) | balance per section, correlation, mono warnings |
| Is there boom where the picture has none, or mud? | `look_sound` (`what: "low_end"`) | each track's and the mix's share under 150 Hz, under 60 Hz and in the low mids (150–500 Hz, where parts piling up sound muddy) |
| What is the tempo, is it steady, do sounds sit on the beat or the document's tempo map? | `look_music` (`what: "beats"`) (`stems: true` for every stem) | on a render, the document's tempo checked against the audio: "124 BPM, the document's; the audio's beats fit it" (or "do NOT fit it", with what they read). A groove also repeats at half, double, 2/3 and 3/2 of its tempo, so the reading from the sound alone (`heard_bpm`) can name a related tempo; the summary says so. With `wav` (no document), the audio's own BPM, and a close call names the other reading. Also `steadiness`, beat and downbeat guess, per onset the ms from the nearest 1/4 beat (+ = late) against the audio's own beats and the document's grid (swung where the document swings); `swing`: the swing set (each track's feel) next to the swing heard, on the same 0 … 1 scale ("swing set 0.55 on 8ths (drums), heard 0.56"); picture: waveform, beats, onsets coloured green / orange / red |
| Is a melody on the right notes, in time and in tune? Are a chord part's notes all there? | `look_music` (`what: "pitch"`) with `track` | every written note (each note of a chord too) judged on its attack, what is new in the sound just after it starts, so a ringing harp, a pedalled piano or a reverb tail never reads as extra, missing or late notes: ok / wrong_octave / missing, and on a one-voice part also late / early (from the sound's onsets), out_of_tune and wrong_note (from the pitch line). The first line says the register in plain words: "bass sounds where written (68 of 70 notes heard in their octave)" or "bass sounds one octave lower than written on 54 of 69 notes (written D3, heard D2): the instrument's samples, or your octave". Wrong octave is called only when the partials clearly say so (a low note's strong 2nd harmonic is not mistaken for it). Plus the pitch line's notes (name, cents off, vibrato, drift): on a ringing part it holds more notes than were written; those are tails and are not counted. What it can't tell: a written note that is an overtone of a note sounding with it (an octave or a twelfth above) reads as there. Picture: the pitch line over the written notes, a dot per written note |
| Do two tracks fight for the same frequencies? | `look_sound` (`what: "clash"`) (needs stems) | spans ranked by how far one track buries the other (`buried_track`, `buried_by_db`, at least `min_over_db` 3): when, which Hz band, which two tracks, dB each stands over the other; an empty list is "no real problem" (equal levels in a band are not a clash); picture: time × band map coloured by pair. The floor follows the mix (reply's `floor_db`); tighten with `within_db` 3 and a `floor_db` 10 dB higher if a dense mix lights up everywhere |
| What key and chords is it in, and are the written notes playable and clean? | `look_music` (`what: "harmony"`) (no render needed: it reads the written notes; only a song with no notes, `source: "audio"` or a `wav` reads audio) | key (the song's own key when it has one, and the summary says so: "Key D minor (the song's key)"; only without one, the notes' or audio's estimate), chord per bar or beat, and plain-fact checks on the written notes: out of the instrument's range, close intervals under the low limit, voices crossing, upper voices over an octave apart, notes out of key (the summary names them: track, note, how many, from when) |

Read the numbers as measurements: the engine compensates recorded instruments' attacks so a note at a marker is heard at it, and `inspect_instrument` gives each one's `attackMs`; onset detection itself reads a soft sound some ms after it starts (measured 1–11 ms for brass and harp, more for a very soft start) (a sound already playing at 0 s counts as an onset at 0), the chord from audio is a best
match to common shapes (a mix with drums and a melody on top reads rougher than the notes written in the document), a
key estimate cannot always tell a major key from its relative minor (the tool says when both fit), and the chord check
reads a written note as there when it is also an overtone of a lower note sounding (an octave or a twelfth above it), so
a note played an octave low passes; a very low note (under about E2) can read missing when its sample has little
fundamental: listen there before changing it.

What these cannot tell you: whether an instrument sounds real or sampled, whether phrasing is musical, whether a
recording sounds like the object on screen. Say so in your report; a person decides those by listening.

## Effects against a picture

- `look_video` `what: "sheet"` to see the whole clip, `"motion"` for sudden-stop candidates (impacts), `"frames"`
  around a time to check one event frame by frame.
- `inspect_library` (`what: "cuts"`) cuts recordings into single sounds with facts (peak, attack, brightness, low-end share), cached,
  so you can pick a cut by its measurements and place it with a clip (`in` = the cut's start).
- After placing sounds, `look_music` (`what: "events"`) with the event times as `expect` checks sync within a frame.
